# 05: Eric checks the clock on real machines and the projector

**What to do:** This is a step only Eric can do: check the built clock on the browsers and hardware teachers actually use, which the automated review (04) can't fully cover. Do it before sharing the link with other teachers.

Spec: `.scratch/school-clock/spec.md`.

**Blocked by:** 04

**Status:** open

1. On your MacBook, open the clock (local preview or the deployed `mrhsieh.app/clock`) in **Safari**, then **Chrome**, then **Firefox**. In each:
   - Click "turn sound on", turn Bells on, start a 1-minute timer, and switch to another app. Check it rings on time and the screen flashes.
   - Set a Clock Alarm 2 minutes ahead, put the tab in the background behind other windows, and check it rings on time.
   - Go fullscreen, leave it alone for longer than your screen-sleep setting, and check the screen stays awake.
2. In **Chrome**: open the mini player, drag it over Slides or Docs, and check it stays on top and updates. In Safari and Firefox, check the mini player button isn't shown.
3. Project it in your classroom. From the back row, check you can read the big Readout and the Segment name, and that the flash is noticeable but not unpleasant.
4. On your phone, open the clock and check it's usable.
5. To check a state without waiting for it, add `?at=` to the URL, e.g. `mrhsieh.app/clock?at=2027-01-20T09:50` (HS Experience Week) or `?at=2026-11-04T11:03&speed=30` to watch a Passing Period and its Bells go by quickly. A "Simulated time" banner shows while spoofed.
6. Write anything that felt off under `## Comments` (or tell Claude in chat).

- [ ] Checked in Safari, Chrome and Firefox on the MacBook
- [ ] Mini player checked in Chrome
- [ ] Projected and readable from the back of the room
- [ ] Checked on a phone
