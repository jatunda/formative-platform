School Clock: three mockup directions

Static, non-functional mockups with fake 2026–27 data, for picking a direction (ticket 02). Every frame below is the real page, live and scaled down, at the sizes from the self-review checklist. Open a direction to click through every state with the switcher in its bottom-left corner, or address a state directly, e.g. a/index.html#passing&theme=light. Add &clock=24 for the 24-hour time setting. Full-size screenshots: run node .scratch/school-clock/mockups/shoot.mjs and look in shots/ (not committed). Notes from the self-review are in REVIEW.md.

A · Monument

Open A

A dark wall clock. One huge, light-weight number in the middle, the Segment name above it, a hairline progress bar of the Segment with its start and end times, and nothing else competing; the controls are a row of quiet text buttons along the bottom. Optimises for reading the number from the back of the room and a calm projected view. Trade-off: the least context: you see now and up next, never the shape of the whole day, and the thin digits lose some punch on a washed-out projector in the light theme.

B · Timeline

Open B

Schedule-aware, like a departure board. A condensed signage face sets the number left-aligned and very large, an Up next card sits beside it, and a strip of today's Segments runs along the bottom with a Now marker, so the class can see where the day stands (on a phone the strip becomes an agenda list). Breaks are hatched and Assemblies dotted, not just recoloured. Optimises for situational awareness: exact times, what's next and how the day is shaped, all at once. Trade-off: the busiest projected view, and the strip needs width; it is the most to look at.

C · Dial

Open C

A progress ring around the number, with rounded type and soft tiles beside it for Up next, extra Readouts and the timer. Long countdowns read in whole minutes ("42 minutes left"), switching to m:ss under 10 minutes; the ring carries the sense of time draining away. A Passing Period dashes the ring. Optimises for a friendly, at-a-glance feel of "how much is left", and an easy place to grow extra Readouts. Trade-off: no seconds on long countdowns, and the ring costs width, so four-digit values (a 10:12 clock) are smaller than in A or B.
